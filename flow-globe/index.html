An origin-destination map for Power BI. Where from, where to, how much — and it draws the network on a 3D globe or a flat map, from the place names already in your data.

Five questions, one map

A flow map is easy to make spectacular and hard to make useful. Flow Globe is built around the five things a reader asks of a network, and gives each one its own visual channel.

  • Where does it run?

    Arcs follow the great circle between two places, on the globe or on a flat map. Places are sized by their total activity.

  • Which way?

    Particles or arrows travel along each flow. When two places trade in both directions, show both arcs or net them into one.

  • How much?

    Thickness carries the value, never glow — in the What changed view, the larger of the two periods. The legend gives the range; each flow's card gives the figure and its share of the network shown.

  • Against plan?

    With an objective, colour turns to variance: above or below plan, how far, and which corridors have no objective at all.

  • What changed?

    With last period's value, every corridor is classed as appeared, gone, up, down or stable — or unknown when the two cannot be compared. A corridor that vanished is still drawn, at the volume it used to carry.

The views

The view menu, at the bottom left, switches what the map shows. The Value view colours by category; Variance to objective and What changed unlock as soon as their field is added; and each extra measure or category you add — up to two of each — becomes a view of its own, named after your field.

Read a place, read a flow

Hover gives the essentials; a click pins the full card, which you can move out of the way. A place card gives inbound, outbound and net flow, its connections and its rank. Turn on Dependency analysis, in the Format pane, and it adds how many places feed it and how many it serves, its top five origins and destinations with their shares, and — with Premium — its concentration indices. And whenever a destination depends on a single origin, the card says so.

Built for real networks

  • Levels that follow the zoom

    Continents, countries, then cities and ports. Or fix the level yourself.

  • Bundled corridors

    In the Value view, flows that share an endpoint and a heading can travel together on a common trunk, to untangle a hub.

  • Keep what matters

    Show the largest routes only, set a minimum value, or — with Premium — keep just the corridors that carry a chosen share of the volume.

  • Search

    Type a place or a flow to find it, select it and fly to it — also the keyboard way into the map.

It tells you what it left out

When the visual cannot place a name, merges rows, filters flows or leaves rows out, it says so: a place name it does not know, a name shared by several real places, coordinates that contradict the name, flows beyond the render limit, a value that is not a number. Each notice says what was affected and what to do about it.

A Diagnostics panel counts it all — input rows, valid routes, routes drawn, places not recognised — so that the figure on the map can always be traced back to the data.

Nothing leaves the report

The world map, the place database and the fonts are inside the visual. It makes no network call of any kind and declares no privileges, so it behaves the same in a locked-down tenant as on a laptop without a connection — one of the conditions Microsoft sets for certifying a visual.

The privacy page sets out what that means in detail.

For every reader

  • Ten languages

    English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese — country names included. It follows the report's language by default.

  • Beyond colour

    Optional hatching and dots mark unfavourable, falling and missing flows as well as colour does, and they switch on by themselves in high-contrast mode.

  • Dark and light

    Two themes drawn for the page they sit on: dark for a dark report, light for a light one.

  • Calm by request

    Every control works from the keyboard, and animations stop when the system asks for reduced motion.
