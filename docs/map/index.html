Map and cards

Moving around the map, choosing the level of detail, keeping the flows that matter, and reading the cards.

Globe or flat map

3D shows an orthographic globe: drag to turn it. 2D shows a flat map: drag to move it. In both, scroll or use + and − to zoom. On the flat map, the Path setting in the Format pane chooses between the Shortest route (the great circle, curved on a flat map) and a Direct line.

When the report opens, the camera frames the flows. Lock view, in Options, stops any automatic reframing; the Format pane card Camera also has Fit on load and Fit on selection.

Levels of detail

Flows can be grouped by Continents, Countries or Cities/Ports. In the default Auto display mode, the level follows the zoom, and so do the layers — borders, labels, country names. It never goes coarser than the level where there is still something to draw, so flows inside a single country do not vanish at country level.

Switch Display mode to Manual, in Options, to fix the level with Group by and to choose the layers yourself.

Keeping what matters

SettingWhat it does
Routes shownKeeps the N largest flows. all by default.
Minimum valueHides flows below a value.
Cover % of volume PremiumKeeps only the largest corridors that together carry the chosen share of the total. At 0 it is off.
Render limitIn the Format pane. How many flows are drawn at most — 2,000 by default. The largest are always drawn first.

Filtered flows are not lost: Diagnostics counts them, and the panel at the top left gives their number whenever it is open. A flow's card gives its share of the flows shown, and a place card adds its share of the full network when filters hide part of it.

Reciprocal flows

When two places send flows to each other, Split draws both arcs. Net replaces them with a single arc in the dominant direction, as thick as the difference; its card gives the volume in each direction. Net needs a Value.

Bundling

Around a hub, many flows leave in the same direction. Bundle gathers flows that share an endpoint and a heading — within a 60° cone — onto a common trunk. The toolbar button switches it on and off; the Bundle flows slider in Options sets how far it goes, and Bundle around in the Format pane chooses the end they gather around: the origin, the destination, or the busiest of the two. Paths become schematic, and the visual says so. Bundling applies to the Value view only.

Cards

Hover is light, click is complete. A click pins the card; drag it by its header, or move it with the arrow keys once its header has focus (Enter puts it back), and close it with Esc.

A flow

On hover: origin → destination, the value and its share of the flows shown, what the current view encodes — the category, the variance, the change or the extra field — and the columns of the Tooltips well. The click card adds the objective or the previous value, the distance, the rank among the flows shown, the full category breakdown, and the list of other flows that pass under the pointer at the same spot. On a small visual, cards keep only the essentials.

A place

On hover: total activity and its share, the line of the current view, inbound and outbound bars in the Value view, and internal flows if any. The click card adds the bars in every view, the net flow, the number of connections and the rank.

Dependency analysis

Turned on in the Format pane, card Nodes; off by default. The click card of a place then adds, across the full network: the destinations it serves and the sources that feed it, its top five origins and destinations with their shares, and two indices Premium: the share of its five largest flows in both directions (Top 5 concentration), when it has more than five, and its inbound concentration (HHI), given as High, Medium or Low. Whenever a destination is fed by a single origin, the card shows a Single origin alert, whether or not the analysis is on.

Report page tooltips

To use your own Power BI tooltip page instead of the built-in hover card, set Rendered by to Power BI (report page tooltips) in the Format pane, card Hover, and configure the page under General › Tooltips.

Selection and cross-filtering

  • Click a flow to select it: the other visuals on the page filter to it.
  • Click a place to select all of its flows.
  • Click a bundle trunk to select every flow it carries.
  • Ctrl-click (⌘ on a Mac) adds to the selection; a click on empty space clears it.
  • Right-click opens the Power BI menu — drill-through, include, exclude.

When another visual highlights part of the data, the flows concerned stand out and their card gives the highlighted amount.

Search

The magnifier opens a search box. Type part of a place or of a flow; the list updates as you type. Choose a result with the mouse or with ↑ ↓ and Enter: the map flies to it — unless Lock view is on — and it is selected, as a click would. A place or flow hidden by your filters is listed as not shown; choosing it still filters the other visuals. The first entry, Reset view, returns to the default view of the globe and forgets the saved camera position. Search covers every place and every flow up to the render limit; names that were not recognised are listed but cannot be chosen.
