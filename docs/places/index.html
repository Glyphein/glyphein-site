Place names

How Flow Globe turns the names in your data into positions on the map — without any online service.

Flow Globe carries its own place database. Nothing is looked up online: names are matched inside the visual, in the report, in a fraction of a second.

What it knows

  • 250 countries and territories, by name or by ISO code (two or three letters). Country names are recognised in English and in their French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese forms, as well as their official long forms.
  • More than 32,000 cities of over 10,000 inhabitants, from GeoNames, and the world's major ports. City names are also recognised in Japanese, Chinese and Korean script.
  • Common alternative spellings and the usual names of capitals.

Matching ignores case and accents. City-states such as Singapore or Monaco resolve to the city.

Places that share a name

When several real places share a name, the most populous is used, and a notice lists the names concerned. To choose another:

  • put your country column in Origin country or Destination country; or
  • write the context into the name: Toledo, Spain, Paris, TX; or
  • give the coordinates.

If the country you give contradicts the place found, the visual keeps the place it knows and says it lies outside the given country or region. A two-letter state code that matches nothing is not flagged: Hamburg, PA resolves to Hamburg, Germany. When in doubt, use the country column or coordinates.

Places it does not find

A name it cannot place — a warehouse code, a village, a misspelling — is listed in a notice, unrecognised places, and counted in Diagnostics. Its flows are not drawn. Use a country, a major city, a port or an ISO code, or give coordinates.

Coordinates

The four latitude and longitude wells place a point exactly, and take priority over the name. Use them for sites, depots or anything below city level.

  • Decimal degrees, latitude within ±90 and longitude within ±180.
  • (0, 0) is read as a missing value: leave those cells empty instead.
  • If the coordinates lie more than 2,000 km from the place the name designates, the visual warns that latitude and longitude may have been swapped.
  • A latitude without its longitude is ignored, and the place is located by name.
  • A point given only by coordinates takes the name of the nearest city, when there is one close by. To show your own label, use the display name wells.
