Getting started

Add Flow Globe to a report, place three fields, and read the map.

Add the visual to a report

Once Flow Globe is published, it is added like any visual from Microsoft AppSource: in the Visualizations pane, choose Get more visuals and search for Glyphein Flow Globe.

If you were sent a .pbiviz file, choose Import a visual from a file in the same menu instead. The visual's icon then appears in the Visualizations pane; click it to place an empty Flow Globe on the page.

Draw a first map

Three fields are enough:

  1. Origin — a column of place names: countries, cities, ports or ISO codes.
  2. Destination — the same, for where each flow arrives.
  3. Value — a measure, such as a sum of amounts, tonnes or shipments. It sets the thickness of each flow.

The map appears as soon as Origin and Destination are filled. Without a Value, each row Power BI sends — one per distinct combination of the fields — counts as one, and the visual asks for a measure. Places are found by name inside the visual — there is no latitude or longitude to prepare. The chapter Place names explains how.

What you are looking at

Arcs
One per origin–destination pair. Thickness is the value; the legend gives the range. Small particles travel along each arc to show its direction.
Places
A marker at each end. Its size is the place's total activity — everything that leaves it and arrives at it.
Legend
Bottom right. It says what colour and thickness mean in the current view. The arrow at its top collapses it.
Level
Zoomed out, flows are grouped by country, or by continent; zoom in and they resolve to cities and ports. Country labels are written in capitals.

The controls

The controls sit along the bottom edge, so that the map keeps the whole frame; zoom is on the right edge, and notices appear at the top left.

ControlWhat it does
View buttonsBottom left. Value, then Variance to objective and What changed, then one button per extra field. A view whose field is missing is shown greyed, with the reason. See The views.
OptionsThe sliders icon after the view buttons. Routes shown, minimum value, bundling, theme, level of detail, language, layers — and the Diagnostics panel. See Settings.
Hide controlsThe eye, bottom right. Hides the controls for a clean screenshot; click again to bring them back.
SearchThe magnifier. Type a place or a flow to find it, select it and centre the map on it.
BundleGathers flows that share an endpoint and a heading onto a common trunk, and ungroups them.
3D | 2DGlobe or flat map.
+ and −Zoom in and out. On the right edge.

On the map itself: drag to turn the globe or move the flat map, scroll to zoom. Hover a flow or a place for its figures; click it for the full card. Clicking also selects it, which filters the other visuals on the page — see Map and cards.

When the visual is narrow, controls give way one after the other, least useful first. The Options button is always the last to go.

Next steps

  • Add an Objective measure to see which corridors run under plan.
  • Add a Previous value measure to see what appeared, went, grew or shrank.
  • Add a Category, such as a transport mode or a carrier, to colour the flows.
